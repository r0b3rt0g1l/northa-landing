A website isn't bought like furniture: you keep using it, changing it and paying for it for years. Before choosing a vendor —us or anyone else— ask these ten questions and **get the answers in writing**.

## 1. Whose name is the domain registered under?

The domain is your business's address on the internet. It should be registered to you or your company, with access to the account where it's renewed.

## 2. Will I have access to the code and the hosting?

Even if you never touch them, you need to know where your site lives and who holds the keys. If you switch vendors tomorrow, that information keeps you from starting over.

## 3. What happens if we stop working together?

Ask what you get when the relationship ends: code, content, databases and access. An orderly exit says a lot about how a vendor works.

## 4. How and how often are backups made?

"We have backups" isn't enough. Ask how often, where they're stored and how long it would take to restore your site if something fails.

## 5. How fast does it load on a phone?

Most of your visitors arrive on their phones. Ask to see the site's score in [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and what they'd do to improve it.

## 6. Can I update content without depending on you?

If you'll publish news, prices or photos often, you need a simple admin panel. If every change goes through the vendor, every change will take time.

## 7. What does maintenance include, and what costs extra?

Security updates, monitoring, content changes, improvements: ask for the list of what's included and the price of what isn't.

## 8. What happens to my customers' data?

If your site has forms, you're collecting personal data. Ask where it's stored, who can see it and how the privacy notice is published.

## 9. Is the site accessible?

People with low vision, who navigate by keyboard or use a screen reader are customers too. Ask whether the site follows the **WCAG** accessibility guidelines.

## 10. How will we know it's working?

A website should bring in messages, calls or sales. Ask what will be measured (for example, WhatsApp clicks or form submissions) and how you'll see those numbers.
